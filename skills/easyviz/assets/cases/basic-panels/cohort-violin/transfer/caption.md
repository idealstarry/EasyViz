Participant BMI distributions in four selected source cohorts from Massier et al., Nature Communications (2023), Fig. 8a (DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-36983-2; source release https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/y3pxvr4xbf/2; CC BY 4.0). All 185 available values in Krieg (23), Petrus (29), Kerr (77) and Arner, E (56) are retained; none of these selected records has missing BMI. The other four source cohorts are outside this declared display. Violins use Gaussian KDE with Scott bandwidth and 100 evaluation points, trimmed to each cohort's observed range with independently normalized widths. Width does not encode sample size. Inner filled boxes show raw-scale, linearly interpolated Q1 and Q3, with a dark median segment and no whiskers; these are descriptive quartiles, not confidence intervals. Every raw observation is drawn with categorical-only beeswarm placement, preserving BMI on the 15–60 kg/m² axis. No pairing, adjusted comparison or hypothesis test is inferred; source field names are remapped for this transfer only.

A thin neutral violin boundary stays subordinate to the colored IQR area and graphite median. Opaque graphite raw observations occupy an adjacent right-hand beeswarm lane. This categorical offset is a presentation choice; it introduces no additional groups or change in BMI values.

The KDE body is unfilled; categorical color is confined to the inner Q1–Q3 area. The cohort mapping matches the companion box view and adapts selected PROGENy Fig. 4c PDF area fills to cohort quartile summaries, without adopting that reference layout or biological encodings.

The sampled source regions include inner summaries and colored density/pathway areas; applying all selected colors to cohort IQR regions is an EasyViz adaptation.
