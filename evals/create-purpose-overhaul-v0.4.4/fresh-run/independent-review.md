# Independent review of the frozen cell-number panels

The with-skill export has a concrete local benefit: its separate observation and summary lanes make the 18 means and SEM intervals easier to identify. The baseline remains useful and has advantages in numerical resolution and condition decoding. These two outputs do not establish an overall winner or general Create effectiveness.

## Source, statistics and export checks

- The three frozen task/source input hashes match. Every artifact listed in both delivery freezes also matched before and after this audit.
- Both plotting-data tables retain all 156 source records, source cells, labels and numerical strings exactly. There are 18 population/condition groups with the adopted counts, original population order and condition order.
- Means, sample SD and SEM were independently recomputed with Python `statistics`, using all values in each group. Baseline metadata matches exactly; the maximum with-skill difference is 1.11 × 10⁻¹⁶. SEM is sample SD divided by √n. This check authenticates against the supplied CSV, not the uninspected original workbook.
- Each actual PDF contains 156 observation paths, 18 mean strokes and 18 mean ± SEM interval paths. All observation centers and interval endpoints match the independent values and the documented coordinate organization, with maximum positional error below 0.00005 pt. No raw glyphs intersect one another or extend beyond the data region in either PDF.
- Both actual PDFs are 119.9999966 × 59.9999983 mm, within PDF extraction precision of 120 × 60 mm. Their 17 readable text spans are ArialMT at exactly 8 pt, embedded, and within the page. Both SVG canvases are `340.15748pt` × `170.07874pt`, with editable Arial text. No text clipping was visible in either full PNG or 96 dpi PDF render.
- The baseline PNG is 1417 × 708 pixels; the with-skill PNG is 1417 × 709. This one-pixel floor/round difference is normal raster quantization of the requested physical canvas. It is not evidence of visual cropping, superiority or failure. The directly inspected vector canvases and glyph geometry are the relevant evidence here.

### Concrete baseline limitation

The full PNG and the 96 dpi PDF show summary strokes interrupted by hollow observations. The independent PDF footprint test finds 47 observation glyphs touching at least one summary stroke across the 18 groups. The observations are drawn after the summary and have white interiors, so they cover parts of the interval or mean stroke. Examples are the crowded SPM and MG groups and the low +DT SILPM/CLPM groups. This is a raw/summary visibility problem, not missing observations or incorrect statistics. It is relevant because comparing central values and uncertainty is part of the adopted reading task.

The baseline's mean ± SEM remains recoverable from the black strokes and caption, and its horizontal scale makes raw-value differences more spacious. Its circle/square condition mapping is also explicit without relying on color alone. Those advantages should not be discarded when interpreting the local summary-visibility issue.

### Concrete with-skill benefit and tradeoffs

The actual with-skill PDF has zero raw-glyph/summary-stroke footprint intersections. The short colored mean stroke is stronger than the capped SEM line, and the observations occupy an adjacent lane. I can follow a condition's raw cloud and its summary without having to disentangle a black interval running through the observations. This supports quick comparison of increased or decreased +DT group means within each population.

The nine-column organization spends horizontal space on categorical grouping, so small numerical differences receive less physical separation on the shorter vertical scale. Four recurring elements per population—gray points, gray summary, blue points, blue summary—require readers to follow both the category label and the color. With no separators or value grid, the compact repeated arrangement gives less guidance for fine value estimation than the baseline. The same circular shape is used for both conditions. These are observable tradeoffs, not reasons to infer that the with-skill result is universally better.

## Bounded conclusion

Both frozen exports correctly implement this supplied task at its adopted dimensions and typography. The with-skill result provides a meaningful improvement in separating raw observations from mean ± SEM summaries; the baseline offers greater value-axis resolution and redundant condition shapes. The evidence supports that specific reading benefit on this one task. It does not establish a causal effect of the skill, superiority across basic charts or datasets, improvement over other models, or journal/CNS readiness. The source normalization denominator, mouse identities and experiment batches remain unavailable; neither panel supplies new inference. No calibrated print or monochrome proof was inspected.

No trial artifact, plotting runtime or README was modified. This review and its own independent renders/evidence are the only added files.
